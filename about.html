About Nurture Impact

Over 30 Years Across the Youth & Community Development Sector

Meet Alan Curtis

Alan has over 30 years of experience across Ireland's Youth & Community Development sector, spanning a broad range of organisations, programmes and areas of community and youth development work.

Within that career, Alan spent approximately 20 years working within Pobal, managing major programmes, reviewing thousands of applications, and seeing first-hand what separates successful funding submissions from the rest. This provides specialist expertise in funding, programme management, assessment and Pobal funding applications in particular.

Alongside this funding expertise, Alan brings deep governance experience built over decades of board service, senior leadership, and sectoral stewardship. He has supported voluntary boards, charities, and social enterprises through restructures, compliance challenges, strategic planning, and organisational change.

He understands the pressures facing boards and organisations today: regulatory expectations, risk management, financial oversight, board dynamics, funding readiness, and the need for clarity of roles and responsibilities.

Through Nurture Impact, Alan brings this combination — broad sector experience, deep specialist Pobal knowledge, and practical consultancy and training experience — directly to the community groups, social enterprises, and voluntary boards who need it most.

The Nurture Impact Approach

Alan founded Nurture Impact because he saw too many good organisations struggle with funding — not because their work wasn't valuable, but because they didn't know how to present it in a way that appraisers could score highly.

The same was true for governance. Boards wanted to do the right thing but felt overwhelmed by compliance requirements and jargon-heavy guidance.

Nurture Impact exists to bridge that gap. Every masterclass is built on a simple principle: practical skills that can be used immediately. No theory for theory's sake. No death by PowerPoint. Just clear, actionable guidance from someone who's been on both sides.

What Guides the Work

  • Clarity over complexity – If it can't be explained simply, it's not ready to teach
  • Tools, not just talk – Every session includes templates and resources participants can use straight away
  • Respect for the sector – Community organisations are doing vital work with limited resources; training should recognise that reality
  • Long-term capacity – The goal isn't dependency; it's helping organisations stand stronger on their own
